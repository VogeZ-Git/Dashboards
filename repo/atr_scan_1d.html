WHAT THIS IS

The purple ladder from the ATR model, for every ticker at once. Six levels; level 6 is the all-time high since the anchor date and the rest are fixed fractions of it. The page answers the question the chart makes you do one symbol at a time: which names are sitting on a level right now, and what is the next one below.

THE LADDER

Straight from the indicator's inputs - these are not fitted, estimated or optimized here:

Level 61.00 x the anchored high
Level 50.77 x - the shallow buy zone, roughly a 23% pullback
Level 40.63 x - the deep buy zone, roughly a 37% pullback
Level 30.50 x
Level 20.38 x
Level 10.23 x

Despite the name, nothing here is an average true range. It is a percentage ladder hung off the high, and the high is a running maximum - so the whole ladder steps up the day a ticker makes a new high.

THE ANCHOR DATE IS THE WHOLE GAME

The ladder is only meaningful if the anchor window contains the all-time high. In the source video this is handled by hand: a stock that listed after the anchor shows no levels at all, and one whose real high predates the anchor shows levels that are quietly wrong.

Here it is checked. The anchor is clamped forward to each ticker's first bar, and any ticker whose pre-anchor high exceeds the anchored high is listed in the amber block at the top of the scanner. Those rows are wrong, not approximate. Re-run with an earlier --start.

EVERY COLUMN

ZONEWhich two levels price sits between. Context, not a signal - "between L5 and L6" only means price is less than 23% off its high, which is most names most days.
ON LEVELThe signal. Price is within 3% of that level. The video buys at the line, not anywhere in the band above it. L5 and L4 are the two it names as good buying in an uptrend.
REGIMELONG or FLAT from the daily trend model, read out of regime_1d.json. TRADEABLE means sitting on level 5 or 4 AND the daily trend is LONG - the video's sentence, enforced: "level five and level four typically good buy zones if we are in an uptrend".
FROM ATHDrawdown from the anchored high, in percent.
SUPPORTThe nearest level below price, and how far down it is. This is where a limit-buy goes.
RESISTANCEThe nearest level above. In the multi-model video this is the main use of the ladder: a level overhead is a reason to sell a call or trim, not to buy.
L5 / L4 / L3The price of those levels and the move needed to reach them. The column is boxed when price is currently in that level's zone.
HELD BEFOREFor the nearest level below: how many times price approached it and how often it held. See below - this is a coarse measure, not a probability.
ATH AGEBars since the anchored high. A large number with a small drawdown means a long grind sideways under the high.

HOW "HELD BEFORE" IS MEASURED

An approach is counted when price closes within 1.5% of the level having previously been at least 3% away, so one visit counts once rather than once per bar. It held if price then rose 5% within 20 bars without first closing 3% below the level.

Read this as a chart note, not a statistic. It answers "has this line ever meant anything on this chart" - the video's advice to "look for what levels have historically been strong in the past". With a handful of approaches per level, 3 out of 4 is not evidence of anything. It is also computed on closes, because the shared price cache carries opens and closes and no highs or lows.

HOW TO USE IT

The workflow in the source material, in order: thesis on the high timeframe, then levels as the entry zone, then the other models for confirmation.

1. Filter to TRADEABLE - within 3% of level 5 or level 4 with the daily trend still long.
2. Put the first tranche at the level, not at the current price. The video buys in thirds: at the level in zone, at the next one down, and one held back for a deeper break.
3. Cross-check on the confluence tracker and the MR scan. A level 5 that also has price below the lower Bollinger band and a z-score past its band is the "six or seven things telling you the same picture" case.
4. A level overhead with mean reversion stretched is the covered-call setup, not an entry.

WHAT IS NOT HERE

The ATR model's own buy and sell signals, and its noise-suppression dial, are not reconstructed - they are not in the input block this page was built from, and guessing them would produce a number that looks authoritative and is not. That also matches the presenter's own advice for accumulation, where he turns the signals off and uses the levels alone.
