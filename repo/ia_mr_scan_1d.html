WHAT THIS IS

The IA-MR mean-reversion model run across the whole watchlist at once, with each ticker on its own optimized Z-Len and Band. The chart shows one symbol; this shows all of them, and adds the number the chart cannot: the price at which each ticker would reach its band.

THE MODEL

A z-score of price against its own moving average, smoothed, with signals taken at turning points beyond a band:

ZHow many standard deviations price sits from its Z-Len-bar average, then smoothed with an EMA(7).
BANDThe per-ticker threshold. Z at or beyond +band is stretched high, at or beyond -band is stretched low.
SIGNALNot simply crossing the band - Z must make a turning point beyond it. A pivot of strength 2: the extreme must be more extreme than the 2 bars either side of it.

A signal is always 2 bars late, and that is not a flaw to fix. A turning point cannot be identified until enough bars have formed after it. So the FIRED column marks a pivot that was confirmed on the latest bar but actually occurred 2 bars ago, at a price you can no longer get.

That is exactly why the FORMING and BUY @ / SELL @ columns exist - they are the forward-looking part.

EVERY COLUMN

Z-LEN/BANDThis ticker's preset. Marked fb when no preset exists and the generic fallback is being used - treat those rows as unoptimized.
ZCurrent smoothed z-score, with a bar showing where it sits between the two bands.
ZONEBUY ZONE z is at or below -band. SELL ZONE at or above +band. NEUTRAL in between.
FIREDA confirmed signal on the latest bar - the pivot itself was 2 bars earlier.
FORMINGZ has turned beyond the band but the pivot is not yet confirmed. This is the early warning. It can evaporate: if the next bars push z further, no pivot forms there and no signal appears.
BUY @ / SELL @The forward-looking numbers. The price at which the next bar's z would reach -band or +band. A dash means no plausible price gets there in one bar, which is normal for a long Z-Len.
TO BANDHow far z is from its nearest band, in z units. Negative means already beyond it.
LAST SIGDirection and bars since the most recent confirmed signal.
B/STotal buy and sell signals over the loaded history - a sanity check on whether the preset produces a sensible trade rate on this ticker.

HOW BUY @ AND SELL @ ARE COMPUTED

Not by rearranging a formula. A new bar changes the moving average and the standard deviation as well as the numerator, so the relationship between price and z is not linear. The smoothing is unwound first - the EMA recursion gives the raw z the next bar must print - and then the price producing that raw z is found by bisection against the actual window.

They are not signal prices. Reaching the band puts z in the zone. A signal additionally needs a pivot, which needs 2 more bars. Read these as "the level where this becomes interesting", not "the level where it triggers".

THE OPTIMIZED SETTING

The PINE PRESET / OPTIMIZED toggle switches every row between the Z-Len and Band from the original Pine table and a setting found by grid search here.

The search runs each (Z-Len, Band) combination through a backtest using the actual trading rules: LIFO lot matching, 60% of available cash deployed on each buy, 60% of the open position sold on each sell. Fills at the signal bar's close, matching the setting the Pine presets were produced under. Cells are ranked on the equal-weight percentile blend of return and win rate, and the pick is the median of its 3x3 neighbourhood so a result that only works at one exact setting is discarded.

Only the first 70% of history is used to choose. The remaining 30% is scored afterwards and never influences the selection. The optimizer report prints both, and the honest number is the out-of-sample one - specifically OOS EDGE, the return minus simply buying and holding over the same stretch.

Win rate is a weak objective on its own here. This is mean reversion: it buys weakness and sells strength, so it wins often almost by construction. Across the grid the median correlation between return and win rate is about +0.6 - they usually agree, but where they disagree the high-win cell is frequently the lower-return one, because taking a profit constantly while the residual position bleeds still books a win every time. That is why the default ranking blends the two rather than maximising either.

And the honest caveat. Per-ticker optimization was tried three times on this same watchlist with the Bollinger model, with progressively stricter validation, and every time the out-of-sample edge was indistinguishable from noise. Nothing here proves this run is different. Read the OOS columns before you switch the toggle and leave it there.

LIVE PRICES

When a live feed is attached, PRICE updates and Z is re-estimated from it, along with ZONE and the distance to the band. The Z-Len average and the standard deviation stay fixed at the last completed bar, and FIRED never moves - a signal needs a confirmed pivot on closed bars.

CAVEATS WORTH KEEPING

The presets came from an ROI optimization on this same signal engine. Nothing here re-validates them, and a parallel exercise on the Bollinger version of this watchlist found that per-ticker optimization produced no out-of-sample edge at all. Treat the presets as a reasonable starting configuration, not as evidence.

Scanning 39 tickers means the extremes on this page are partly selection. A few names sit near a band on any given day whether or not anything is happening.

Used well, this is a shortlist generator: it tells you which of your names are stretched right now and where the interesting levels sit, so you can look at the chart for the handful that matter instead of all 39.
