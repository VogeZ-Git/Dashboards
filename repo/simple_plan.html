status review — 15-11-2026

The plan still holds. Nothing in the last five days breaks its logic, and the one number it tells you to watch is comfortably inside its line. Four things changed underneath it, and three of them create a dated decision.

1. The safety line on concentration is crossed, and the fix is the overdue order. The Elon bag is ~59% of the book against a 45% cap. The plan's own answer to that is the TSLA tranche sequence — and TA, the first one, is the order that did not get placed on 08-11. The bulletin's default is to fold it into 22-11. That default is the single live item on this page. Allocation check 15-11: the tranche sequence on its own gets the bag to ~50%, not under 45% — see the pink rows above.

2. A dated forced seller has appeared in MSTR, and the plan has no line for it. MSCI has confirmed Strategy's index deletion, effective the 30 November close, with JPMorgan sizing index-fund selling at ~$2.8bn. MSTR is ~11.1% of the book. The bulletin records this as an open decision with a default of no action, hold to 02-01. That default is unchanged by the news — but it is now being taken against a confirmed and sized seller on a known date, instead of a possibility. Deciding to do nothing is still a decision; this page exists so it is a deliberate one.

3. The CRCL exit has effectively switched routes. The plan offered two ways out: a spike, or the 15-01 backstop. With BTC at $68.1k and below all three of its key pricing levels, and the CRCL trigger at $95–103, the spike is no longer the likely path. Plan for 15-01 as the route that happens, and treat a spike as the surprise rather than the plan.

4. The merger question that hangs over the 06-12 tranche has gone quiet. Prediction markets moved to ~8.5% for a merger announcement in calendar 2026, down from ~20% at end-October; Tesla's annual meeting on 3 December carries no merger item on the ballot; and Bloomberg reports a push to announce in H1 2027. Terms landing before 06-12 now look unlikely, so TC should be expected to proceed as written.

What the "wait until 02-01" rule has cost and saved, stated honestly. The three names the plan defers are NVDA, memory and AMZN. Since 10-11: NVDA has fallen through a founder-CEO resignation to a sixth straight down week (the wait saved money so far), while MU rose +12.8% in the week to 13-11 and Goldman lifted its target to $1,250 (the wait cost money so far). That is the rule working as designed — it trades upside for not having to be right under pressure. Both legs are recorded here so the trade-off stays visible rather than being remembered selectively.

Macro, for context only — it changes nothing on this page. October PPI came in at 6.5% year on year, the highest since November 2022; the 10-year reached 5.16%; prediction markets put ~90% on a December hike, with Fed speakers openly discussing two more or 50bp. A plan whose rules are "no borrowed money, no new buys until 02-01" is the right shape for that backdrop, which is the main reason nothing here needs rewriting.
