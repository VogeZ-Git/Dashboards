10Δ Framework Backtest

Rolling the 10Δ covered call indefinitely · NVDA · MSTR · IBIT · TSLA · companion to the 10Δ Decision Framework

Read this before you trust a single number

No historical option prices are used anywhere in this page. Closed-contract option quote history is not free and was not available when this was built. Every premium, strike and delta below is reconstructed with Black-Scholes from a model of implied volatility. The underlying share prices are real (daily closes from the ConfluenceTracker cache); the options are not.

So this tests whether the rules are internally coherent and how sensitive the outcome is to assumptions — not what your fills would have been. The two IV models sit side by side precisely so you can see how much of the answer is the strategy and how much is the assumption:

  • Realized-vol model — IV tracks a blend of 20d and 60d realized vol times a volatility-risk-premium multiplier, with a skew haircut for OTM calls. IV expands when the stock moves, which is what funds a roll during a run-up.
  • Flat IV model — one constant IV per name, no skew, no term structure. A stress test, not a best guess: it removes the vol expansion that rescues the roll in a parabolic move, and the results show how much that rescue is worth.

Known omissions, all of which flatter the strategy: no gap risk (prices move close to close only), no real bid/ask beyond a flat modeled spread, no early assignment, no margin or borrow cost, no tax, and perfect execution at the modeled mark every single week.

One knob dominates everything once a position gets stuck: the spread cap. It binds only on expensive deep-ITM contracts, and a position that has gone deep ITM is rolled every single week forever under the no-assignment rule — so the toll compounds into the dominant term. On a five-year NVDA run the same rules give +44% frictionless, −112% at a 15-cent cap and −868% at a one-dollar cap, against +1044% for simply holding. Set the cap to what you actually pay before reading any long-window result as a verdict.

Framework rules

The ITM branch follows the "When the position goes ITM" rule: keep rolling out for a credit while raising the strike, assignment never accepted. Taken with no cap that rule has a degenerate answer — jump to the longest expiry for a one-cent credit and collect nothing for a year — so the search stops at the shortest expiry whose best credit-clearing strike is actually above the old one, and Max roll DTE bounds how far it may stretch. That cap moves the result a lot: try 60 against 120.
